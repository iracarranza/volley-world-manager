# Club spaces, visits, information, transfers, and cozy management

Design record for the discussion beginning with parameterized regional office appearance and continuing through facility visits, information access, club/academy career structure implications, free agency, manager-to-manager interaction, and the cozy-game identity of VWM.

Each item below is intentionally short: one or two lines capturing the addition without over-specifying implementation.

## Office and facility appearance

- **Regional appearance is a grammar, not one fixed room.** Each region keeps its canonical floor/wall/desk construction while individual offices vary within bounded material parameters.
- **Useful appearance parameters:** value, saturation, pattern strength/scale, wear, finish, plus material-specific controls such as timber grain/plank width, stone joints/slab variation, and plaster mottling/patching.
- **Parameter ranges are region-specific.** A'ace should remain polished and low-wear even at its rough end; Ĭspayk should remain repaired, aged, and matte even at its refined end.
- **Individual offices combine several causes.** Regional architecture + club resources + club character + building age + occupant/history should produce variation without erasing regional identity.
- **Wealth and prestige are visually separable.** An old prestigious club can be worn but expensive; nouveau riche can be pristine but newly manufactured or stylistically cheap.
- **Spaces can change over time.** Promotion, financial crisis, ownership change, repairs, and long-term history can alter condition or furniture while persistent pieces preserve continuity.

## Leaving the player office

- **The player office remains the home interface.** Routine management, reports, correspondence, planning, and broad oversight should be fastest there.
- **Other locations provide depth rather than replacing menus.** Medical bays, training courts, staff rooms, other club offices, arenas, and regional/federation spaces exist because presence changes context and access.
- **Travel should not become walking-simulator friction.** “Visit club/facility” should normally be an intentional scene transition, with the destination itself carrying the meaning.

## Information geography

- **Information exists where it is produced, then travels to the manager.** The office aggregates staff knowledge; visiting the source gives more immediate, contextual, or granular evidence.
- **Information can vary by accuracy, completeness, and freshness.** A staff report may be highly accurate but a day old; an in-person observation may be immediate but narrower and more subjective.
- **Visiting should not reveal secret true numbers.** Specialists can still know more than the manager; presence adds context, recent changes, conversation, and firsthand observation rather than omniscience.
- **Staff quality includes communication quality.** Better staff can produce faster, clearer, more appropriately uncertain reports, reducing the need for the manager to inspect everything personally.
- **Firsthand information should be experiential.** Training visits can reveal role behavior, communication, fatigue, disengagement, pair chemistry, or coaching habits that a summary appropriately compresses.

## Managerial time and attention

- **Time represents attention, not action points to maximize.** The central constraint is that the manager cannot personally be everywhere, not that every hour contains a collectible reward.
- **The office gives breadth; presence gives depth.** Staying at the desk can process many subjects quickly, while a visit spends time to understand a smaller number of things more richly.
- **Delegation must be safe normal play.** Trusting a good physio, assistant, scout, or analyst should usually be sufficient; personal attendance is for curiosity, uncertainty, importance, or relationship value.
- **Most opportunities should persist or recur.** Missing a routine visit should not permanently punish the player; rare truly one-off events can matter because they are exceptional.
- **Information advantages should be gradients, not gates.** Not visiting usually means knowing less firsthand, not being denied information required to play competently.

## Club versus academy structure reaffirmed

- **Club employs; academy selects; home region remains formative identity.** Clubs are everyday institutions where volis live, train, and compete, while academies are regional representative bodies preparing Sixnet squads.
- **The academy is not a youth setup.** Existing encyclopedia/UI language that still implies “academy = young development club” needs a dedicated terminology and encyclopedia overhaul.
- **Club competition is the proving ground for academy selection.** Minutes, role, performance, opponent quality, consistency, and specialist success create evidence for representative selection.
- **Academy selection values marginal squad contribution, not conformity or an ability leaderboard.** Orthodox regional fits and contrary specialists both need demonstrated representative value.

## Free agents and transfer structure

- **Club employment is normal but should not be universal.** Free agents should exist as a real career state rather than forcing every acquisition to be a club-to-club transaction.
- **Free-agent signing and contracted transfer are distinct.** A free agent requires club/manager-to-voli recruitment; a contracted voli requires both club-to-club permission and voli agreement.
- **Transfers have three actors.** The recruiting manager asks what the voli solves, the current manager asks what would make losing them acceptable, and the voli asks whether the new role and life are preferable.
- **The current manager should have roster reasons, not only a price.** Importance, replacement difficulty, finances, competitive relationship, squad depth, and the voli's situation should shape willingness to negotiate.
- **The voli can disagree with both clubs.** A selling club may approve a move the voli rejects, or a voli may want a move their current manager refuses, creating meaningful consequences and relationships.
- **Useful free-agent states include prospect, released, contract-expired, mutually separated, and contracted.** Clublessness can create pressure through loss of housing, care, elite training, and match evidence without an arbitrary desperation modifier.

## Visiting other clubs beyond transfers

- **Visits are a general information-and-relationship action, not a transfer interface.** Transfers are only one downstream consequence of understanding another institution.
- **Observe training and institutional practice.** Visits can reveal intensity, drill structures, role priorities, tactical habits, player trust, retraining, and the causes behind statistical outputs.
- **Learn other managers.** Repeated contact can make their rotation, development, workload, tactical, and interpersonal tendencies more legible without reducing them to one relationship score.
- **Build professional relationships and trust.** Better relationships can improve openness, cooperation, informal information, friendlies, staff exchanges, and future negotiations.
- **Arrange joint activity.** Scrimmages, preseason camps, coaching or analysis exchanges, specialist sessions, shared events, and other cooperation give visits non-transfer purposes.
- **Scout institutions as well as players.** Facilities, accommodation, medical practice, morale, staffing, and operational quality can matter independently of any single signing target.
- **Learn regional volleyball firsthand.** Visiting another region's clubs can show how its philosophy is trained, taught, and adapted beyond what match results alone reveal.
- **Informal information can emerge socially.** Staff changes, dissatisfaction, tactical shifts, role changes, or facility problems may become visible through access, but should remain partial rather than omniscient.
- **Access is asymmetric.** Reputation, relationship, rivalry, timing, and purpose determine whether the player receives a public tour, open training, closed sessions, staff discussion, or no access at all.
- **Trust partly means access.** A strong relationship can open increasingly private institutional spaces without making friendship an automatic gameplay bonus.
- **Visits can support knowledge diffusion.** Ideas can spread through observation, collaboration, hiring, and repeated competition, letting volleyball culture evolve between clubs and regions over long saves.

## Cozy identity and success

- **Cozy must be a mechanical constraint, not only an art style.** A large possibility space becomes stressful if every unchosen action reads as lost value or failed optimization.
- **The core fantasy should be cultivating a volleyball place worth caring about, not merely becoming “the best club.”** Winning is one valid expression of a successful save rather than its sole answer.
- **Success should be plural.** Sporting achievement, relationships, player development, club culture, facilities, regional contribution, institutional reputation, history, and collections can all support long-term play.
- **Players should be allowed not to care about some systems.** Staff and priorities can surface the areas a manager values while competently handling the rest.
- **Slow noncompetitive accumulation is important.** Office/facility personalization, memorabilia, photos, shirts, gifts, records, persistent wear, and other career artifacts can make the club feel increasingly like the player's place.
- **Relationships can be rewards in themselves.** Not every visit, dinner, conversation, or friendship needs an explicit negotiation modifier or resource payout.
- **VWM does not need one final victory condition.** Sixnet wins, championships, academy selections, long-serving players, staff trees, club relationships, and distinctive institutional reputations can accumulate into a living club history.
- **Losses should become history, not proof of incorrect calendar play.** Missed signings, departures, defeats, and failed qualification can be meaningful events without implying that the player failed to optimize enough actions.
- **Time-system design rule: presence creates texture, not mandatory efficiency.** Going somewhere should offer different experience, context, or relationships rather than being strictly superior to staying home.
