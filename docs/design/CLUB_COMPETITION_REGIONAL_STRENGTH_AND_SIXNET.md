# Club competition, regional strength, and the Sixnet

Design record. This document formalizes how clubs, regional philosophies, regional strength, transfers, and the Sixnet relate. It complements `SIXNET_ZAITGAIST_AND_TACTICAL_COHERENCE.md` and `TRANSFER_MOTIVES_AND_TACTICAL_FIT.md`.

## 1. Clubs are the everyday expression of a region

Clubs compete by gathering the volis they can recruit and demonstrating their home region's volleyball philosophy through those people.

A club is not a miniature academy and does not need to employ only locally developed volis. Its roster can include imports, role specialists, stars, reserves, and tactically unusual fits. What remains regional is the institutional context in which those volis are asked to play:

- coaching assumptions;
- tactical principles;
- training habits;
- what kinds of roles the club commonly values;
- the wider developmental culture around the club;
- the local competitive and economic environment.

Therefore:

```text
REGION
produces a volleyball tradition and player-development distribution
        ↓
CLUB
recruits actual available people
        ↓
MANAGER / CLUB TACTIC
asks those people to express the club's regional philosophy
        ↓
MATCH RESULTS
become evidence about how well the region's volleyball works in the world
```

This lets clubs within one region differ substantially without ceasing to be regional representatives.

## 2. Regional strength emerges from club and player evidence

Regional strength should not be a direct rating assigned to a region.

It is evidence accumulated from the region's actual volleyball ecosystem.

The intended interpretation is:

> How well is this region currently demonstrating that its volleyball philosophy, institutions, and available talent can win?

Relevant evidence includes:

- number and depth of strong clubs;
- club results, especially interregional results;
- quality of the region's player-development output;
- standout volis;
- positional breadth;
- quality of an ideal representative starting six;
- quality of the 6–10 useful reserve/substitute options behind that six;
- recent Sixnet prestige, kept separate from underlying volleyball strength.

Regional strength therefore sits between the club season and the Sixnet:

```text
CLUB SEASON
    ↓
club strength + club results + player evidence
    ↓
REGIONAL STRENGTH
    +
recent Sixnet prestige
    ↓
REGIONAL STANDING
    ↓
Sixnet qualification / seeding
```

## 3. Imports can strengthen two regions, but through different evidence channels

A cross-regional transfer should not force one answer to the question "which region gets credit for this voli?"

The cleaner rule is that the voli can contribute to both regions, but not for the same reason.

### Playing region: institutional / club evidence

If a Spëddigh-raised middle plays for a Taktikã club and that club wins, the result is evidence for Taktikã's regional ecosystem.

Taktikã supplied the club, manager, institutional context, tactical framework, teammates, preparation, and competitive environment that turned the roster into a winning side.

Therefore:

```text
club result
→ belongs to the club's region
```

Imports are part of the resources a club successfully or unsuccessfully assembled.

### Home region: talent / representative evidence

The same middle remains a product and representative candidate of Spëddigh.

Their current ability, reputation, positional quality, and suitability for the Spëddigh academy can therefore strengthen Spëddigh's talent and representative-squad case even while they are employed abroad.

Therefore:

```text
voli's formative/home-region talent value
→ belongs to home region

academy eligibility
→ belongs to home region
```

### Why this is not necessarily double counting

The same person appears in two different questions:

```text
Did Taktikã's clubs succeed with the people they assembled?

How strong is the talent Spëddigh can call upon for representative volleyball?
```

Those are both true pieces of evidence.

The implementation should avoid simply adding one undifferentiated "player value" to both regions. Instead regional strength should preserve component provenance, for example:

```text
CLUB / INSTITUTIONAL COMPONENT
club strength, depth and results
→ credited to club region

TALENT / REPRESENTATIVE COMPONENT
home-developed volis and ideal academy squad
→ credited to home region
```

This produces useful world dynamics:

```text
minor region develops elite voli
        ↓
major-region club recruits and develops them further
        ↓
major region gains club success
        ↓
home region gains a stronger academy candidate
        ↓
those regions may later oppose each other in the Sixnet
```

## 4. The Sixnet removes club advantages and tests regional philosophies directly

Club competition is intentionally messy.

Clubs differ in:

- money;
- accommodation;
- roster depth;
- star treatment;
- recruitment reach;
- managers;
- staffing;
- local vacancies;
- imported talent;
- history and status.

The Sixnet should feel categorically different.

Once regions qualify, the competition selects representative academy squads from the strongest eligible volis and asks each region to express its volleyball directly.

The conceptual transition is:

```text
CLUB COMPETITION
"What can this institution build with the people it can get?"

        ↓ regional standing selects the field

SIXNET
"What does this region's volleyball look like when it can call its best representatives?"
```

The Sixnet therefore strips away much of the club-level noise:

- club wealth does not directly decide the match;
- club housing does not decide the match;
- club roster hierarchy does not carry over;
- imported volis return to their own representative eligibility;
- academy selection concentrates the region's tactical identity.

This is why the Sixnet is a direct clash of philosophies rather than a championship between the richest clubs.

## 5. Court phenomena should reinforce that distinction

The court-phenomena system can make Sixnet fixtures feel like regional volleyball entering physical space.

The intent is not that regional identity grants arbitrary hidden outcome bonuses. Rather, the venue/court phenomena can make the clash of traditions legible and memorable at the world layer.

Club fixtures show institutions trying to make regional volleyball work with heterogeneous rosters.

Sixnet fixtures show those regional philosophies in their most concentrated representative form.

## 6. Club competition should not require home-and-away geography

Clubs need a reason to play in regions where neither side is local.

The cleanest world explanation is that top-level club volleyball operates partly as a travelling interregional circuit rather than as a conventional territorial home-and-away league.

Possible fixture contexts include:

- hosted series weekends;
- invitational meets;
- regional festival rounds;
- neutral interregional cups;
- rotating circuit stops;
- major venues that host several clubs over a short block;
- showcase events attached to scouting, commercial, cultural, or civic events.

Thus a fixture can be:

```text
Taktikã club
vs
Spëddigh club
hosted in Blôc du Larg
```

without either club representing Blôc du Larg.

The host region is the venue, not the competitive identity of either side.

This structure has several advantages:

- creates neutral-court conditions without inventing exceptions;
- exposes clubs and managers to more of the world;
- lets court phenomena appear during ordinary club seasons outside a club's home region;
- gives scouting and transfer activity natural geographic overlap with fixtures;
- avoids requiring every club pair to play a balanced home-and-away schedule;
- supports a shorter, curated fixture calendar rather than a huge league grind.

The player-facing calendar can distinguish:

```text
HOME HOST
your club / region is hosting

AWAY HOST
opponent's club / region is hosting

CIRCUIT / NEUTRAL HOST
third region or designated event venue
```

## 7. Neutral venues should matter physically, not competitively by fiat

A neutral-region fixture should not give the host region's clubs or philosophy an automatic tactical bonus when they are not playing.

What the host can legitimately affect is the environment:

- court phenomena;
- venue geometry/presentation where applicable;
- travel/recovery context;
- crowd composition and local attention;
- scouting visibility;
- event prestige.

The competing teams still carry their own club-region tactical context.

So:

```text
HOST REGION
→ place / environment

CLUB REGION
→ institutional and tactical identity

VOLI HOME REGION
→ formative inheritance / representative eligibility
```

These three provenance labels must remain distinct.

## 8. Regional strength should preserve provenance rather than become one opaque sum

A single public standing may ultimately be necessary, but internally the system should be able to explain why a region is strong.

Useful component families are:

```text
CLUB ECOSYSTEM
How many strong clubs exist and how deep are they?

CLUB RESULTS
How well do those clubs perform against other regions?

HOME-REGION TALENT
How strong are the volis the region has produced, wherever they currently play?

REPRESENTATIVE DEPTH
How strong is the ideal academy six plus reserves?

SIXNET PRESTIGE
What recent incumbency has the region earned at the championship level?
```

This allows two regions to reach similar standing through different structures and gives the player/world news something meaningful to discuss.

## 9. Transfer and Sixnet feedback loop

Cross-regional movement makes the competition ecology recursive:

```text
REGION A develops a voli
        ↓
REGION B club recruits them
        ↓
Region B club improves / wins
        ↓
Region B's club-result case strengthens
        ↓
the voli develops through stronger competition
        ↓
Region A's academy candidate pool strengthens
        ↓
Region A may become a stronger Sixnet opponent
```

This is desirable rather than a loophole.

It means wealthy or prestigious club ecosystems can import talent without monopolizing representative identity.

It also means player development abroad can contribute to the rise of an otherwise thin region.

## 10. Design principles

1. **Clubs represent a region institutionally, not ethnically.**
2. **A club demonstrates its region's volleyball with whoever it can successfully recruit and integrate.**
3. **Club results belong to the club's region.**
4. **A voli's representative/talent contribution belongs primarily to their home/formative region.**
5. **The same voli may therefore strengthen two regions through different causal channels.**
6. **Regional strength emerges from evidence; it is not assigned by lore.**
7. **The Sixnet is a representative clash of regional philosophies, not a club championship.**
8. **Neutral-host club fixtures are part of a travelling interregional circuit, not geographical contradictions.**
9. **Venue provenance, club-region provenance, and voli-home-region provenance must remain distinct.**
10. **Do not collapse the component model into opaque double counting when implementation begins.**
