# Traits

The master list, reviewed against what the engine can actually express today.

## The principle

> Traits should tell the player what kind of system fits that voli best. Traits
> should not dictate exactly what a voli will do, but inform what types of
> actions they are natural at.

That is the whole design and it has one implementation consequence worth stating
before the list, because it decides whether an entry is cheap or expensive:

**A trait is a bias in a decision the engine already makes. It is never a new
branch.** If a trait adds a branch, the resolver grows a special case per trait
and sixty traits is sixty special cases that interact. If a trait shifts a
weight in a decision that was already being made, sixty traits is sixty numbers
and they compose for free. Every entry below is classified by which of those it
is, and the ones that need a genuinely new action are marked as such because
they are the expensive ones and there should be few of them.

The corollary, which the list already half-states with its `*` convention: a
trait describes what a voli **reaches for**, never what they **achieve**. That
distinction is load-bearing and is discussed on its own below.

## The state of `traits` today, stated plainly

`VolleyballPlayer.traits` is an `Array[String]`. It is read in exactly two
places in the codebase and four trait names exist anywhere:

| trait | generated by | read by | live? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ambidextrous` | `familiarity_system` | `familiarity_system` | yes |
| `Functional Weak Hand` | `familiarity_system` | `familiarity_system` | yes |
| `Early Riser` | **nothing** | `daily_schedule_system` | **no** |
| `Night Owl` | **nothing** | `daily_schedule_system` | **no** |

Half the existing traits are a threshold outside its own distribution: the day
model shifts a training window by four or five blocks for a trait no voli can
have. That is `FAILURE_MODES.md` §0 in miniature, and it is the first thing to
fix — not because those two matter, but because **the same defect will be
reproduced sixty times if traits stay as free strings**.

Three structural problems follow from that, and they need solving before any of
the list is typed in:

1. **Strings are not identifiers.** Adding a trait today means typing the same
   string in two files and hoping they match. There is no registry, no
   completeness check, and nothing that fails when a trait is written but never
   read — which is exactly how `Early Riser` got into this state.
2. **Nothing generates traits except one hand-preference branch.** There is no
   trait assignment in `player_generator` at all, no per-region affinity, and
   no budget. Sixty traits with no generator is sixty inert strings.
3. **"often/rarely" is one signed axis, not two traits.** Half the list is
   polar. Stored as two strings a voli can hold both, the list doubles, and
   every read site has to check for two names. One id carrying a signed
   magnitude reads once and cannot contradict itself.

## The mechanism most of this list should be built on

`SystemFitProfile` already exists, is live, and is read at five sites in the
resolver. Every profile answers three questions about one player and one
tactical quantity: what value do they naturally want (`ideal_value`), how much
deviation can they absorb (`tolerance`), and did they land in the tight band.
Four axes exist today — approach distance, set release interval, block
engagement distance, defensive depth — each derived from attributes.

**That is the principle at the top of this document, already built.** "What
system fits this voli" is literally what a fit profile answers. So the natural
shape for traits is not a parallel system but a layer on this one, and the
taxonomy falls out:

| channel | what a trait of this kind does | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. shifts an ideal** | moves a fit profile's `ideal_value` | free — the site already reads it |
| **B. widens or narrows a tolerance** | scales `tolerance` | free, same reason |
| **C. biases a choice** | changes a weight in a selection the resolver already makes between options that already exist | cheap — one weight per decision site |
| **D. biases a technique** | picks which technique is attempted inside a situation the engine already classifies | medium — needs a technique layer that does not exist yet |
| **E. new action** | the action itself does not exist | expensive |
| **F. outside the rally** | day, food, room, mood; never touches the resolver | cheap, but mostly waits on unbuilt systems |

Channels A, B, C and F should be the overwhelming majority. D is one new
concept that unlocks a whole group. E should be a handful, and every E entry
should be justified by the action being worth having for its own sake.

## Six findings before the list

### 1. `*` — "attempts to" — is the best thing in the list and should be a first-class property

A trait that says *attempts a pancake* and a trait that says *pancakes
successfully* are different objects. The first is a preference and belongs here;
the second is an attribute wearing a trait's clothes, and the moment it exists
the trait and the attribute disagree about the same voli.

So the `*` should not be a footnote in a document. Every trait in channels C, D
and E carries an **attempt** side and never an outcome side, and the outcome is
resolved by whatever attribute already governs it. `*uses pancake receives`
means *reaches for a pancake in situations where one is possible*; whether it
comes up is `ball_control` and `improvisation`, unchanged.

This also makes traits legible in exactly the way the principle asks for. A voli
who attempts pancakes and misses them is telling you something true about what
system suits them — and it is more informative than a voli who simply has good
hands.

### 2. Several entries are attributes that already exist, and duplicating them is a defect

| entry | already modelled as | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| gets winded easily | `stamina`, through `FatigueModel.winded_fraction` and `WINDED_CAPACITY_LOW/HIGH` | **drop.** It is continuous, built, and a flag on top of it produces two sources of truth for one fact |
| uses sky ball serves | `primary_serve_style`, an enum that already contains `"Sky Ball"`, with `serve_style_proficiencies` behind it | **drop.** Already a property |
| uses hybrid serves | same enum, already contains `"Hybrid"` | **drop.** Already a property |
| jump sets when fatigued | your own note is right: this is `stamina` × `work_rate` × the fatigue stage, all three of which exist and all three of which already decide whether a jump is available | **drop as a trait**; it is the fatigue model being visible |
| has unnaturally short/long arms/legs | limb proportions are in the physical profile and `BODY_TYPES.md` | **keep, but invert** — see below |

The body one is worth its own rule, because it generalises: **derive the trait
from the body, do not stamp the body from the trait.** A voli whose arm length
sits in the tail of the generated distribution gets the label; the label does
not go on first and then move the arms. Otherwise generation and the trait
system each have an opinion about the same centimetres, and the §0 pattern
repeats — a knob acting on a quantity somebody else already set.

Applied to the whole list, that rule retires "gets winded easily" and "gets
stretched often" as *inputs* and re-admits them as *labels on outliers of
something already modelled*, which is both cheaper and more honest.

### 3. The RARE / RESTRICTED tier is doing something categorically different, and should be named differently

Look at what is actually in it: *recovers stamina between sets*, *keeps form
while unbalanced*, *can jump and attack with no approach*, *sets 0 tempos*,
*completely guesses for blocks*, *chases down tools and wipes*.

None of those are preferences. They are **capabilities** — things a voli can do
that most volis cannot, which either open an option that is otherwise closed or
remove a penalty that otherwise always applies. Balancing a preference and
balancing a capability are different jobs: a preference that is slightly too
strong makes a voli slightly odd, and a capability that is slightly too strong
makes a voli mandatory.

They should be a separate word and a separate budget. Traits are common,
several per voli, and describe fit. Capabilities are rare, at most one per voli,
and describe what they can do at all. Mixing them in one list means the rarity
of a capability is enforced by a generation weight rather than by the type
system, and that is the kind of thing that quietly drifts.

### 4. Two families are distributions, not flags

*Gravitates to line / short / deep / cross / cut* and *loves to set the outside
/ middle / opposite / pipe* are each written as a menu of separate traits. They
are one trait each: a weighting over a set the engine already has.
`CourtConstants.LANES` is the setter one exactly. Storing them as five flags
lets a voli gravitate to both line and cross, which means nothing, and makes the
generator's job five times harder for no expressive gain.

Same shape, differently: *looks for line / cut / tool* on attack.

### 5. Receiving technique is one new concept that unlocks a whole group

The engine already classifies how strained a contact was — `contact_posture` is
one of `planted`, `reaching`, `off-axis`, `moving`, and `contact_recovery` is
one of `platform`, `knee`, `fall`. Both are *derived from the situation*, which
is right.

What does not exist is a **technique** layer: given that the situation is
`reaching`, what does this voli reach with. Tomahawk, pancake, one arm, rolling
— all four of the starred receiving traits are answers to that one question, and
none of them need new geometry, new timing or a new outcome model. They need one
selection between techniques that are legal in the situation the engine has
already named, weighted by trait, resolved by attributes that already exist.

That is a single medium-cost addition that turns four expensive-looking entries
into four numbers. It is the highest-value item in the whole list.

### 6. Some of this list has nothing to attach to yet, and should be marked rather than built

- **Allergies, overeating, homesickness, socialising, hypochondria** all belong
  to accommodations and care, which is 495 lines of design reaching one label on
  the journal. Under the club design these become load-bearing — they are how a
  voli's club preference gets its shape — but there is no system to bias yet.
- **Chases down tools, wipes, bombas** is off-ball movement, which has just
  gained its first honest phase targets and does not yet have a chase model
  beyond the second contact.
- **Attacks from outside the court** needs the court boundary to mean something
  to the attack geometry, which it currently does not.
- **Tool, wipe, bomba** are not in `docs/textbook/GLOSSARY.md`. Terms the
  simulation branches on should be defined there before they are coded.

## The list, classified

Channel key: **A** shift an ideal · **B** scale a tolerance · **C** bias a
choice · **D** bias a technique · **E** new action · **F** outside the rally.
Status: **wire** = the read site exists · **weight** = needs one weight at a
site that exists · **new** = needs a mechanism · **waits** = needs an unbuilt
system · **drop** = already modelled elsewhere.

### General

| trait | channel | touches | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| unnaturally short / long arms | — | physical profile, reach, block reach | **label on an outlier**, not an input |
| unnaturally short / long legs | — | `stride_length_m`, and through it the approach fit ideal | **label on an outlier** |
| gets stretched rarely | F | injury/rehab; `REHAB_BLOCKS` exists in the day model | waits |
| allergies | F | food, `ACCOMMODATIONS_AND_CARE.md` | waits |
| gets winded easily | — | `FatigueModel.winded_fraction`, `stamina` | **drop** |
| *capability:* recovers stamina between sets and rallies | F | `GameManager` rally-fatigue accrual, `stamina_fatigue_scale` | wire |

### Behavioural

Every one of these is channel F, and together they are the voli side of the club
preference model — what a voli wants from where they live. That makes this the
group with the least engine risk and the most design leverage.

| trait | touches | status |
|---|---|---|
| wakes up early / late | `TRAIT_WINDOW_SHIFT` — the read site **already exists and has never fired** | wire (this is the free one) |
| overeats / undereats | food blocks, condition | waits |
| gets homesick often / rarely | `home_region` vs `club_region`; the fields already distinguish | waits |
| hypochondriac / doesn't report symptoms | injury reporting — no injury model | waits |
| needs to socialise often / rarely | club social time, per the club design | waits |
| changes tactics easily / slowly | **B** — `adaptability` already drives every fit `tolerance`, and familiarity gain is already scaled per region by `read_rate` | wire |

## What that classification says

Counting the list as classified: roughly **five entries should be dropped**
because the thing they describe is already modelled continuously and a flag on
top of it creates a second source of truth. Roughly **eight are already wireable
today** — the read site exists and is inert, `Early Riser` being the clearest.
The **large majority are one weight at a decision site that already exists**,
which is the outcome the "bias, never a branch" rule was chosen to produce.
**One new mechanism — receiving technique — pays for four entries at once** and
is the thing to build first. Only a handful genuinely need a new action, and
each of those should be justified by the action being worth having anyway.

## Order

1. **Make traits a type, not a string.** A registry with an id, a channel, a
   signed magnitude and the site it acts on; a test that fails when a registered
   trait is read by nothing, which is what would have caught `Early Riser`. This
   is small and everything else depends on it.
2. **Split traits from capabilities**, with separate budgets and separate
   generation. One capability per voli at most.
3. **Wire the inert ones** — the day window first, since its read site has been
   waiting since the day model was written.
4. **Collapse the polar pairs and the menus** into signed axes and weightings
   before any of them are typed in, not after.
5. **Build the receiving technique layer.** Four entries, one mechanism, and it
   makes the most-watched phase in the game visibly different between two volis.
6. **Then the weights**, group by group, each measured against the distribution
   of the quantity it acts on before its magnitude is chosen.
7. **The behavioural group last, with the club design**, where it stops being
   flavour and becomes why a voli picks you.

## Not decided here

- How many traits a voli carries, and whether the count varies by region. There
  is no trait generation to base that on yet, and it should be chosen after the
  registry exists, from a measured distribution rather than a guess.
- Whether traits are visible immediately or discovered — which, given the
  whiteboard's *figures, not sentences* rule and the same show-don't-tell
  argument, probably means **observed frequency rather than a stated label**: a
  voli who has attempted eleven pancakes this season has told you what they are
  without the game saying so.
