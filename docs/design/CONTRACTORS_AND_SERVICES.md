# Staff, contractors, and services

A companion to `STAFF_AND_FALLIBILITY.md`.

That document explains how **people inside the club** become useful, familiar
and fallible. This document answers a different question:

> **Which work should require hiring somebody into the club at all?**

The game should not turn every professional relationship into a permanent staff
slot, and it should not reduce every outside relationship to an anonymous price
button either.

---

## 0. Three kinds of work

### Staff

Hire somebody because **continuity, judgment and memory matter**.

They are part of the club.

Examples:

- assistant coach;
- scout;
- chef;
- physio/care staff.

### Contractor

Hire somebody for a **bounded piece of skilled work**.

The deliverable matters more than permanent presence.

Examples:

- architect;
- freelance regional scout;
- specialist serving/blocking coach;
- temporary medical specialist;
- opposition-analysis consultant.

### Service

Pay for a **routine result** where the provider's identity is normally not the
interesting part.

Examples:

- ordinary cleaning;
- utilities;
- transport;
- basic maintenance;
- possibly standard catering.

The practical rule:

> **If the interesting part is who this person becomes in relation to the club,
> make them staff. If the interesting part is what they deliver for one
> assignment, make them a contractor. If the interesting part is only that the
> routine gets done, make it a service and automate it.**

This is the staffing version of `DIEGETIC_MANAGEMENT.md`'s rule:
**physicalize decisions, automate maintenance.**

---

## 1. Contracting is not “cheap bad staff”

Do not model contractors as:

> hired staff, but temporary and worse.

The point is a different **knowledge/time relationship**.

A contractor may be better than an employee at one narrow job because they:

- know the local region;
- specialize in that task;
- have no standing club workload;
- bring experience from many organisations;
- can be hired exactly when needed.

Permanent staff are better at different things because they:

- learn the club;
- know the roster;
- remember previous instructions;
- see problems develop over time;
- act without needing a new brief every time;
- may volunteer information before the manager asks.

The core trade is often:

> **external specialization vs internal familiarity.**

---

## 2. Architect: contractor by default

An architect is the clearest case.

The club hires them for a project:

> design this building / renovation under this brief.

When the building is finished, there is usually no reason to pay them every
week for three seasons.

The relationship may still persist:

- prior projects;
- familiarity with the club;
- reputation;
- preferred structures;
- regional expertise;
- whether they are interested in working with the club again.

A large late-game organisation might retain a firm if continuous construction
ever becomes real, but do **not** create a permanent Architect staff slot merely
because the profession exists.

See `HOUSING_WORKSPACE_AND_ARCHITECTURE.md`.

---

## 3. Scouting is the strongest staff/contract comparison

### Freelance / contracted scout

A bounded assignment:

> Spend two weeks in Rhen Tempaol and find me three middles who fit these
> criteria.

Strengths:

- local knowledge;
- tournament/league specialization;
- inexpensive compared with another full salary;
- useful when the club has no knowledge in that region;
- can be genuinely excellent at the narrow assignment.

Weaknesses:

- limited knowledge of your roster;
- limited knowledge of your tactical history;
- does not accumulate the same club familiarity;
- has little reason to proactively find the weird person who is exactly *your*
  kind of voli six months later.

### Hired scout

The long-term payoff is institutional familiarity:

> "I saw somebody you might like."

They understand:

- what the manager means by vague instructions;
- which weaknesses the roster really has;
- which personalities have worked here;
- what housing/region/squad context recruits are entering;
- how much uncertainty the manager tolerates.

This should not make the hired scout globally better. A local contractor can
still know a region far better.

The decision is therefore not:

> 2★ contractor vs 4★ employee.

It is:

> **specific external knowledge vs accumulated internal knowledge.**

---

## 4. Catering vs an in-house chef

This is the other promising prototype because the food system already has
familiarity, regional knowledge and task complexity.

### Catering service

Strengths:

- reliable standard meal;
- scalable;
- low manager overhead;
- no permanent staff salary/relationship to maintain;
- potentially more reliable than a mediocre chef at one simple repeated order.

Weaknesses:

- limited paste customization;
- poor at many separate accommodations;
- little personal knowledge of volis;
- slower to notice concerns;
- less improvisational when supply or special needs change.

### Hired chef

Strengths:

- learns the squad;
- learns specific ratios and accommodations;
- learns the kitchen and local supply;
- can interpret vague complaints;
- can improvise when supply changes;
- becomes part of club life.

Weaknesses:

- salary;
- workload;
- fatigue/failure;
- their own regional/paste familiarity shapes what they are naturally good at.

Important consequence:

> **Outsourcing can be more reliable for a simple task. Hiring becomes valuable
> when the task is complicated and the people matter.**

That fits `STAFF_AND_FALLIBILITY.md` better than a universal staff-quality bonus.

## 5. Assistant coaching is continuity-heavy

Do **not** make a contracted assistant coach and a hired assistant coach two
interchangeable versions of the same slot.

The assistant's value is daily continuity:

- knows yesterday's training;
- knows the tactic;
- knows individual responsibilities;
- knows which voli is struggling;
- knows what the manager said;
- runs the club when the manager spends attention elsewhere.

That is exactly the kind of work that should be staff.

Contract work still makes sense for **specific interventions**:

- serving specialist for three sessions;
- blocking consultant for preseason;
- guest setter coach;
- temporary coach during absence;
- one opposition review.

The contractor contributes a specialist idea. The assistant carries the club's
ongoing memory.

---

## 6. Physio / care

The same split can exist carefully:

### Outside clinic / specialist

- bounded diagnosis;
- second opinion;
- unusual injury/problem;
- specialist treatment;
- no continuous observation.

### Hired care staff

- sees workload patterns;
- notices changes early;
- knows how each voli normally presents;
- coordinates with schedule/training;
- owns recurring care responsibilities.

Again, the contractor may be more expert on one unusual problem. Staff are more
valuable because they know the person over time.

---

## 7. Contract work needs a brief

Contractors should not be magic buttons.

A contract interaction should usually contain:

- **task** — what are they being asked to do?
- **scope** — which region/voli/project/problem?
- **deadline** — when is the deliverable needed?
- **budget** — what can the club spend?
- **relevant context** — what does the contractor actually know about the club?

Then specialist skill/familiarity determines what they can do with the brief.

Do not build one giant generic contract UI before two real consumers prove they
need it. Architect commission is one real consumer; freelance scouting or
catering should be the second before extracting a universal schema.

---

## 8. Familiarity can survive a contract

Temporary does not mean disposable.

A contractor can accumulate a relationship with the club:

- an architect understands what kinds of housing the manager likes;
- a freelance scout learns what "interesting" means to this club;
- a specialist coach knows the current offense from a previous camp;
- a caterer already knows the club's standard order.

This creates a useful spectrum:

> stranger → repeat contractor → trusted outside specialist → possible future
> staff hire, where the profession supports it.

Not every contractor needs or wants to become staff.

---

## 9. Services should stay boring until they stop being routine

A service is not a hidden character generator.

If transport works every week, the player should not pick a bus company and
manage driver morale.

It becomes visible when:

- a service is being chosen or changed;
- it fails;
- the club needs an unusual version;
- a recurring arrangement becomes strategically meaningful.

This preserves the world without turning the manager into an administrator of
maintenance contracts.

---

## 10. This is also a club-growth model

Contracting gives small clubs access to competence without requiring a complete
staff immediately.

Possible progression:

### Small / new club

- standard catering;
- one hired coach/manager doing too much;
- freelance scouting when needed;
- clinic care;
- architect only when a real project appears.

### Established club

- in-house chef because food/accommodation complexity justifies one;
- dedicated scout(s) because continuous knowledge matters;
- assistant coach because manager attention is scarce;
- permanent care staff;
- still contracts specialists for unusual work.

This is better than simply unlocking "Staff Slot 4" because the organisation
changes **why** it hires people.

---

## 11. The core test

For any proposed role, ask:

1. Is there meaningful work every ordinary week?
2. Does continuity with the same people materially improve the work?
3. Does the role gain useful club-specific familiarity?
4. Does their judgment create events/relationships the player should care about?
5. Would replacing them with an outside specialist change the *kind* of service,
   not only its price?

If mostly yes → **staff**.

If the work is skilled but bounded → **contractor**.

If the identity of the provider is not interesting unless something breaks →
**service**.

---

## 12. Implementation restraint

Do not build a universal contractor framework from this document alone.

First real examples should be:

1. **architect** — bounded project, portfolio, quote and brief;
2. then either **freelance scouting** or **catering vs hired chef**.

Once two unrelated systems genuinely share task/scope/deadline/budget/familiarity
needs, extract only that proven common contract.

This follows the same rule as manager appointments and other cross-system
schemas: abstraction should come after two working consumers, not before them.
