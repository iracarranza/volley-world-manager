# Signature Vocabulary

## Status

Design authority draft for the tiered signature system. Named slots that have not yet been designed are intentionally left open rather than filled with speculative mechanics.

This document distinguishes **capability**, **vocabulary**, and **manifestation**. It does not replace the attribute, trait, fatigue, confidence, or rally-authority systems.

## 1. Core Model

Signatures are not ordinary attributes and are not equipped powers.

A Voli's attributes and body determine what volleyball actions are physically and technically possible. A Voli's **signature vocabulary** determines which unusual solutions they have learned, developed, or internalized strongly enough to recognize and express as characteristic actions. Current match state and actual rally geometry determine whether one of those solutions can manifest.

The basic chain is:

`capability + vocabulary + current state + valid volleyball situation -> manifestation`

A Voli may therefore have the raw attributes to perform the underlying action without possessing its named signature vocabulary. Ordinary volleyball remains available; the signature represents an exceptional, recognizable expression of it.

Signatures must remain downstream of authoritative rally truth. They may change decisions, available solutions, movement/reach within their defined semantics, or other legitimate pre-resolution state. They may not fabricate contact, trajectory, or outcome.

## 2. Relationship to Traits

Signature vocabulary is trait-like in that two Volis with similar attributes may possess different characteristic solutions.

It is not identical to the trait system:

- **attributes** describe competence/capability;
- **traits** describe behavioral individuality and preference;
- **signature vocabulary** describes unusual learned/developed solutions available to the Voli;
- **current state and rally context** determine whether those solutions manifest.

A vocabulary entry does not guarantee use or success.

## 3. Tier Structure

Tiers describe increasing specificity and exceptionality, not merely stronger numerical bonuses.

### Tier 1 — States

Broad, comparatively common match states. These may affect many volleyball actions and do not represent a specialized named technique.

#### Focus

**Fantasy:** the Voli is unusually mentally locked in.

Focus should primarily improve the reliability with which the Voli expresses their existing mental capability rather than granting knowledge or anticipation they do not possess.

Candidate effects include:

- reduced decision noise/error;
- improved maintenance of tactical intent under pressure;
- reduced degradation of relevant mental execution from fatigue;
- greater stability in using existing anticipation/read ability.

Focus must not become a miniature Foresight. It does not reveal information unavailable to the Voli.

#### Hustle

**Fantasy:** the Voli overcomes tiredness and finds enough extra effort to keep an actionable play alive.

Fatigue remains authoritative and may contain multiple states that reduce different attributes. Hustle does not remove fatigue or restore energy. For one urgent action it may temporarily mitigate relevant fatigue-caused reductions, allowing the Voli to express more of their underlying movement/reaction/reach capability than their current tired state ordinarily permits.

Potential contexts include:

- chasing a shanked reception;
- pursuing a deflection;
- a setter chasing a bad pass;
- a blocker attempting to close;
- an attacker reaching an imperfect set;
- recovering into coverage;
- defensive pursuit.

Hustle should apply only to a legitimate, near-reachable action opportunity. It must not make arbitrarily unreachable balls reachable.

The underlying fatigue state remains after the action. The exertion may deepen subsequent fatigue if supported by the fatigue model.

### Tier 2 — Techniques

Specific, recognizable volleyball solutions that may or may not exist in an individual Voli's vocabulary. Attributes still govern execution quality and physical feasibility.

Tier-2 techniques must occupy concrete causal niches. Do not fill slots merely to create symmetry between roles.

#### Defense — Pancake

A forward emergency floor-defense technique.

Pancake becomes relevant when the ball is dropping short and in front of the Voli and a normal platform contact is no longer feasible, especially:

- tips;
- block coverage;
- soft block touches;
- short deflections;
- balls dying immediately in front of the defender.

Its defining geometry is **forward/downward floor interception**. It is not a generic low-ball save proc.

The Voli must still recognize the play, move forward, and place the hand beneath the ball before floor contact. Attributes determine whether this succeeds and what rebound results.

A successful pancake need not produce the control of a stable platform; its primary value is preserving a ball that was otherwise about to contact the floor.

#### Defense — Rolling Receive

A momentum-management defensive technique usable across broad movement directions.

Rolling Receive allows a Voli to make a legitimate defensive/reception contact while carrying enough pursuit or impact momentum that they cannot reasonably finish the action upright, then dissipate that momentum through a roll.

Natural situations include:

- lateral chase;
- diagonal chase;
- hard-driven ball absorption;
- pursuit outside stable defensive posture;
- reception/dig where stopping before contact is infeasible;
- other physically valid multi-directional chase-down contacts.

The technique does not simply increase dive range. Its identity is **contact while moving aggressively + controlled continuation of body momentum**.

The natural cost is positional/recovery debt: the Voli is on or near the floor after the contact and must recover into the next phase.

### Tier 3 — High Signatures

Rare, identity-defining manifestations that create exceptional volleyball solutions. These are not flat upgrades over Tier 2.

#### Foresight — Time

A receiver uses exceptional anticipation and current confidence to begin moving toward an expected endpoint **before the decisive opponent contact occurs**.

The read may concern a seam, trajectory off hands, wipe, serve curve, spike angle, or other developing ball path.

Foresight does not directly buff dig stability. Its advantage is that a correct early commitment gets the defender there sooner and therefore creates more time and better positioning for the eventual defensive event.

Its defining risk is inaccurate prediction. The defender can commit early to the wrong answer.

#### Heroics — Reach / Recovery

A reactive defender produces an extraordinary intervention against a ball that would ordinarily be untouchable or physically overwhelming.

Heroics is associated with explosiveness and work rate rather than calm prediction. Examples include chasing an extreme wipe or absorbing a driven attack with whatever legitimate body contact can keep the ball alive.

Heroics has an exceptionally narrow actionable window. A potential Heroics moment may begin to form but be denied before the extraordinary action becomes possible. A denied Heroics must not fabricate contact.

#### Block Crush — Force

An attacking signature.

The hitter treats meaningful blocking resistance itself as a viable attacking target, using exceptional force and commitment to challenge or overwhelm the formed block rather than avoiding it.

Block Crush is not generic spike power. Its defining event is the contested attacker-ball-block interaction.

#### High Hands — Precision

An attacking signature based on exceptional exploitation of blocking geometry.

The hitter deliberately finds and uses the high/extreme region of blocking hands to manufacture a favorable deflection, tool, or recycle opportunity.

High Hands challenges geometry rather than raw block strength and must remain distinct from Block Crush.

#### Monster Block — Space

A blocking signature in which exceptional reach, positioning, timing, and presence deny an unusually large portion of the attacker's viable attacking space.

Monster Block does not guarantee a stuff. It changes the attacking geometry by removing more solutions than a normal block would be expected to remove.

## 4. Tier Relationships

Lower and higher tiers may occupy related families without requiring a rigid skill tree.

Examples:

- **Hustle -> Heroics**: Hustle temporarily overcomes fatigue/reach loss for a near-reachable urgent action; Heroics addresses an extreme intervention beyond ordinary actionability.
- **Focus -> Foresight**: Focus stabilizes existing mental execution; Foresight is the exceptional case where the Voli commits before decisive truth exists.
- **Pancake / Rolling Receive -> Heroics**: the techniques solve difficult but physically legitimate defensive contacts; Heroics begins where ordinary technique/reach would otherwise fail.

These relationships are conceptual. A lower-tier entry is not automatically a prerequisite for a higher-tier signature unless later design explicitly establishes that rule.

## 5. Vocabulary and Development

Do not assign advanced signatures solely because an attribute crosses a threshold.

Long-term vocabulary development may eventually depend on some combination of:

- sufficient underlying capability;
- traits/preferences;
- repeated behavior;
- training;
- experience;
- successful or formative match events;
- confidence/state history where appropriate.

Two similarly rated Volis may therefore develop different vocabularies because they repeatedly solve volleyball problems differently.

The exact acquisition/development system remains **TBD** and should not be inferred from this document.

## 6. Knowledge / Scouting

A signature may exist in a Voli's vocabulary without being immediately known to the manager or opponent.

Discovery should remain compatible with VWM's broader epistemic design: exceptional behavior can be learned by observing it rather than exposed omnisciently from the start.

Exact discovery thresholds, scout confidence behavior, and UI presentation remain **TBD**.

## 7. Presentation

Signature presentation must annotate the actual extraordinary volleyball action rather than replace it.

Visual treatment should be downstream of authoritative state and contact truth. A signature's identity should primarily be readable through its timing, movement, geometry, and causal behavior; VFX is supporting emphasis.

Detailed VFX authority should live in the dedicated signature-presentation specification rather than being duplicated here.

## 8. Open Slots / Design Questions

- most Tier-2 setting techniques;
- most Tier-2 blocking techniques;
- additional serve/reception techniques;
- additional high signatures;
- exact vocabulary acquisition/development rules;
- whether vocabulary can decay or transform;
- whether some techniques are prerequisites for advanced signatures;
- manager/scout discovery thresholds;
- interaction/counter relationships between vocabulary entries;
- rarity/distribution by competition level;
- whether Tier 1 is formally called `States` in player-facing UI;
- whether Tier 2 is formally called `Techniques` in player-facing UI;
- whether Tier 3 is formally called `Signatures` or another player-facing term.

These slots should remain empty until a mechanic has a clear volleyball fantasy, causal niche, eligibility condition, failure mode, and distinction from existing attributes/traits/signatures.
