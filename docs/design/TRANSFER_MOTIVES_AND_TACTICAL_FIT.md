# Transfer motives and tactical fit

Design note following `SIXNET_ZAITGAIST_AND_TACTICAL_COHERENCE.md`.

## 1. Regional origin is not club destiny

A voli's `home_region` describes formative development and identity. It must not function as a permanent employment preference that makes clubs recruit only local volis.

A Spëddigh-raised middle should be able to build a career in Taktikã, A'ace, Landavol, or elsewhere. Cross-regional movement is necessary for:

- a meaningful transfer market;
- clubs developing identities distinct from their regions;
- tactical synthesis;
- talent drain from minor regions;
- academy selections containing volis who play club volleyball abroad;
- regional styles spreading through people rather than only through abstract drift.

`home_region` should normally remain the representative/academy eligibility region even when `club_region` changes.

## 2. Why a voli transfers

Transfers should result from a bundle of career incentives rather than one tactical-fit score.

Possible motives include:

- role opportunity: starter, rotation role, specialist role, bench, reserve;
- positional vacancy / a club specifically needing what the voli does;
- competitive level and club reputation;
- pay, accommodation, facilities, food, medical/training resources, and general living conditions;
- star treatment / status promises;
- expected playing time;
- pathway to academy/Sixnet selection through meaningful minutes and stronger competition;
- manager reputation and relationship;
- existing teammates / social relationships;
- city/region/lifestyle affinity where represented;
- tactical role fit;
- developmental fit: whether the club can improve attributes or roles the voli values;
- ambition: stronger competition, trophies, visibility;
- dissatisfaction at the current club;
- curiosity or willingness to leave a familiar system, mediated by adaptability/personality rather than nationality.

A'ace should be unusually strong on money, infrastructure, status, and star recruitment, but those motives should exist elsewhere on smaller scales.

## 3. Clubs should recruit functions, not regional labels

A Taktikã club should not ask:

> "Which Taktikã middle is available?"

It should ask:

> "What does our middle role require, and who can perform or learn it?"

Regional development affects the distribution of available qualities, so a Taktikã-raised middle may often be a natural candidate. It is not a hard preference.

Example:

A Taktikã club may want a middle who:

- reads setters well;
- adapts assignments quickly;
- can execute read/commit rules;
- closes enough lateral space;
- provides a credible quick.

A Spëddigh-raised middle with excellent acceleration, tempo comfort, work rate, sufficient block timing, and high adaptability may solve that role differently from a typical Taktikã middle.

The foreign candidate can therefore be attractive precisely because the club is purchasing a capability its local pipeline does not commonly produce.

## 4. Tactical fit has at least three meanings

Do not collapse these into one number.

### Immediate role fit

Can this voli's current physical/technical/mental profile do what the club needs?

This can be high even when their formative tactical inheritance is unlike the club's.

### Tactical alignment

How similar is the club's current system to what the voli grew up regarding as normal?

High alignment reduces relearning cost. Low alignment is friction, not disqualification.

### Tactical learnability

How plausible is it that this voli can become effective in the new system?

Likely inputs include adaptability, tactical familiarity growth, role similarity, pair relationships, and actual training time.

A club may knowingly sign a low-alignment player because the role fit and learnability are strong.

## 5. Natural affinity should be emergent, not regional matching

A "natural affinity for Taktikã" need not be a hidden `likes_taktika` flag.

It can emerge from the voli's actual qualities and temperament.

Examples:

- high adaptability makes unfamiliar tactical systems less costly to learn;
- high decision-making and court vision may suit a read-heavy role;
- high tactical discipline may suit a club that makes explicit assignments;
- lower ego may make repeated role revision easier;
- high improvisation may fit a club asking for recovery solutions when the called structure breaks;
- personal preferences for role/status/training/lifestyle can make the move attractive even when tactical alignment is initially poor.

Therefore a Spëddigh middle can have a natural career fit with a Taktikã club without ever being "secretly Taktikã".

## 6. Foreign recruitment should create both value and cost

Cross-regional transfers are interesting when neither side gets a free conversion.

The club gains:

- a rare physical/technical profile;
- a different tactical prior;
- knowledge of another regional style;
- possibly a solution local development does not provide.

The club pays:

- tactical familiarity time;
- possible role retraining;
- pair-coordination time;
- social/living adjustment where relevant;
- risk that the divergent prior conflicts with interacting teammates.

This supports heterogeneous clubs while preserving meaningful regional development traditions.

## 7. Recruitment can deliberately seek tactical difference

A sophisticated club should sometimes recruit *against* its local developmental profile.

Examples:

- Taktikã recruits a Spëddigh middle because its existing middles read well but cannot close quickly enough;
- Xérvu recruits a Bompaçao passer because aggressive serving culture did not produce enough elite reception locally;
- Pāwa recruits a Taktikã setter because its relentless transition group needs better contextual distribution;
- Blôc du Larg recruits an Ĭspayk opposite because the system already supplies control and needs a terminal outlet.

This is not regional blending as a free bonus. It is roster construction.

## 8. Player choice and club choice are separate evaluations

A transfer occurs only when both sides have a reason.

### Club interest

Potential inputs:

- positional/role vacancy;
- current and projected ability;
- functional fit with club tactic;
- scarcity of the voli's profile in the local/available market;
- age, cost, reputation, potential;
- tactical relearning cost;
- roster status the club can realistically offer.

### Voli interest

Potential inputs:

- promised status / playing time;
- wages/resources/accommodation;
- club competitive level and reputation;
- manager relationship/reputation;
- academy/Sixnet visibility;
- personal ambition;
- current-club satisfaction;
- social connections;
- expected tactical/role fit and retraining burden.

These two scores should not be mirrors of each other.

## 9. Sixnet consequence

Representative eligibility should generally follow formative/home region rather than employment region.

Therefore cross-regional transfers create a useful world dynamic:

```text
minor/other region develops voli
        ↓
foreign club supplies resources, minutes and high-level competition
        ↓
voli improves/reputation rises abroad
        ↓
home region selects them for academy duty
        ↓
foreign club indirectly strengthens another region's Sixnet challenge
```

This is desirable. It makes club economics, talent drain, and regional competition interact instead of treating regions as closed national leagues.

A strong major club can benefit from importing talent while simultaneously helping the imported voli's home region become a stronger Sixnet rival.

## 10. Design principle

> Regional identity should determine what kinds of volis are commonly produced, not where those volis are allowed or destined to play.

Clubs select people for problems. Volis select clubs for careers. Regional tactical inheritance changes the cost and character of the fit, but should rarely answer the transfer question by itself.
