# Gate 43: Causal Attack Preparation

Review date: 2026-07-30

Status: **PASS; ACTIVE IN NORMAL HOME ATTACK RESOLUTION**

Attack preparation is no longer only a playback waypoint. A hitter's perceived
end of ball responsibility and tactical duties determine release time. The
remaining time determines how far the hitter can move toward an approach start.
The resulting position, velocity, facing, balance, acceleration, transition
speed, lateral speed, approach timing, fatigue, and mass determine a takeoff
profile.

That profile changes:

- approach speed and runway completion;
- lateral control and arrival balance;
- the fraction of rated jump available at contact;
- additional takeoff time still required;
- available controlled, placed, power, tip, and tooling actions;
- normal-rally attack quality and fallback attack type.

Authoritative contact truth remains outside player decision input. The hitter
acts from repeated perceived set estimates; truth is used afterward to audit
whether the chosen action can actually be executed.

The regression suite proves that explicit transition release occurs earlier
than a coverage hold, first-contact ownership delays release, a clean aligned
run-up creates more speed and usable contact height than a late reversed run,
and power can disappear while a controlled contact remains available. A seeded
multi-exchange regression also verifies the same contract from floor defense
through counterattack. Normal attack events retain the resolved causal evidence
for playback and diagnosis.
