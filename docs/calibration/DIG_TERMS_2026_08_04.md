# Which term actually moved

Two explanations for the home tilt have now been offered and measured, and both
were wrong. First the parallel floor-defence implementation: fixed, and symmetry
moved *away* from 0.5. Then the arrival-margin timing term: measured, and it
barely discriminates. A composite that only ever reports its product cannot be
asked which factor moved, so `_defense_execution` now reports its factors too,
recorded on every dig event on both sides of the net.

Measured over 290 digs on the roster-cancelling design, with the geometric
attack promoted:

| term | home | opponent |
|---|---|---|
| capability | 0.705 | 0.513 |
| read bonus | +0.047 | **-0.066** |
| timing | 0.720 | 0.520 |
| reach margin | +0.463 m | -0.048 m |
| posture penalty | 0.010 | 0.053 |
| support | 0.010 | 0.005 |
| dig quality | 0.362 | 0.223 |
| **contested against** | **0.257** | **0.375** |

## The dig model is no longer the defect

Posture penalty and support are the terms the last gate made real on the
opponent's side, and they are now near-identical across the net. Those were the
parallel-implementation gaps and they are closed.

Two dig-side gaps remain and both are second-order. The opponent's read bonus is
*negative*: `_defensive_responsibility_fit` returns -0.035 outright for a player
with no assignment, plus a distance term that punishes a defender whose plan
seat sits far from the ball. And capability differs by 0.192 where the read
bonus explains only 0.113, so the *players* doing the digging differ -- the home
claimant search picks by claim score, weighing zone priority, anticipation and
skill, while the opponent falls through to `_nearest_opponent_body` whenever
nobody is reachable, which picks closest rather than best.

## The dominant term is not a dig term at all

`contested_against` is 0.257 for the home defender and 0.375 for the opponent's.
Against `_dig_contest`, which compares dig quality against attack quality plus a
0.20 attacker advantage, that 0.118 gap is worth more than every other row
combined. The home defender is simply being handed an easier ball.

So the question moves off the defence entirely, and the confirming measurement
across all contacts rather than only those that reached a dig says where it
goes:

| | home | opponent |
|---|---|---|
| attack quality | 0.305 | 0.259 |
| **set quality** | **0.484** | **0.254** |

The attack gap is 0.046. The **set** gap is 0.230 -- the largest single
asymmetry measured anywhere in this engine, and it is upstream of everything
else. The opponent's setter delivers half the ball the home setter does, their
hitters swing at what they are given, and the home floor defence spends the
whole match reading an attack built on a bad set.

This is the same defect shape as every other one found in this arc -- one side
modelled fully and the other as a simplified parallel path -- and it was walked
past twice while the defence was being examined, because a dig is where the
symptom shows and the set is where it starts.

# The set, decomposed

Same instrument, one contact earlier. Measured over 488 sets on the
roster-cancelling design:

| term | home | opponent | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| capability | 0.797 | 0.787 | -0.010 |
| usable | 0.828 | 0.764 | -0.064 |
| pass | 0.750 | 0.657 | -0.093 |
| tempo demand | 0.000 | 0.003 | ~0 |
| capability penalty | 0.156 | **0.278** | **-0.122** |
| geometry difficulty | 0.077 | **0.138** | **-0.061** |
| **arrival** | -0.022 | **-0.153** | **-0.131** |
| familiarity | -0.002 | -0.002 | 0 |
| **quality** | **0.512** | **0.225** | **-0.287** |

The prediction recorded before this ran was `pass` -- that the opponent's set is
downstream of an unaudited reception asymmetry. It differs by 0.093 and is not
the largest term. Third prediction, third miss, and the same lesson each time:
the symptom appears one contact after the cause, so reasoning backwards from
where it shows lands one stop short.

## The setter is out of position

`capability` is 0.797 against 0.787. The setters are equal, and so is the model
that reads them -- which rules out both the roster and the attribute weights and
leaves only what the rally hands them.

Three terms carry the gap and all three are the same fact:

- **arrival, -0.131.** `clampf(arrival_margin * 0.18, -0.42, 0.08)` sits at
  -0.022 for the home setter and -0.153 for the opponent's, so the opponent's
  setter reaches the ball around 0.85s late against the home setter's 0.12s.
- **capability penalty, -0.122.** 0.278 against 0.156: the opponent's setter is
  asked for a ball beyond what they can deliver roughly twice as often.
- **geometry difficulty, -0.061.** 0.138 against 0.077: the opponent's sets
  travel harder distances and worse angles.

A setter who arrives late is a setter reaching a ball from the wrong place,
which is what makes the geometry hard and what puts the delivery beyond their
capability. One root, three terms.

And it is the same root the floor-defence gate found: **nobody stages the
opponent**. The home setter is walked to a release target during the first
contact; the opponent's is not, so they run to every ball from wherever the
previous phase left them. The fix that worked for the opponent's six defenders
is the fix this wants, pointed at one more player.
